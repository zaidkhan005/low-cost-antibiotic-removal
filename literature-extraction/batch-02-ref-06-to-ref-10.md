## REF-06 — Ayawei, Ebelegi and Wankasi (2017): adsorption isotherm modelling

**Source role:** General modelling/reference paper; not antibiotic-specific.

**Existing extraction/findings preserved**
- Covers the theory and interpretation of multiple adsorption isotherm models, including Henry, Hill–Deboer, Fowler–Guggenheim, Langmuir, Freundlich and Dubinin–Radushkevich, alongside other models.
- Discusses model regression and error-function/model-fit evaluation. Use this source to explain that isotherm selection involves model assumptions and fit assessment, not simply choosing the model with the largest coefficient of determination.
- Suitable for the isotherm fundamentals and model-interpretation part of the presentation (slide 10).
- The extraction handoff calls for exact equation forms, assumptions, parameter definitions and dimensional checks to be taken from the PDF before the equations are typeset in slides.

**Use qualification:** A general isotherm-modelling source. Do not present it as experimental evidence for antibiotic removal or any particular adsorbent.

## REF-07 — Vareda (2023): validity and physical interpretation of kinetic models

**Source role:** Methodological review/analysis of adsorption kinetic modelling; not a single antibiotic-specific adsorption experiment.

**Existing extraction/findings preserved**
- Discusses pseudo-first-order (PFO), pseudo-second-order (PSO), diffusion-related models and the Elovich model.
- Reviews linear versus nonlinear regression and the implications for model selection and interpretation.
- **Key methodological finding:** PFO and PSO fits can occur in systems limited by surface reaction or by diffusion. A good fit to either common pseudo-order equation is not unique evidence of a specific rate-controlling step and does not, by itself, establish chemisorption.
- Suitable for slide 11 and for qualifying kinetic-model claims throughout the presentation.

**Use qualification:** Preserve the paper's warning against interpreting an empirical kinetic fit as direct mechanistic proof. Distinguish model fit from independently demonstrated transport or surface-reaction mechanisms.

**Locator status:** Page/table/equation locators were not carried over in the transfer record; do not invent them.

## REF-09 — Mirizadeh et al. (2023): raw date-palm waste for tetracycline and ciprofloxacin

**Source role:** Direct experimental study of two antibiotic adsorbates using raw date-palm waste.

**Existing extraction/findings preserved**
- Antibiotics studied: tetracycline (TC) and ciprofloxacin (CIP).
- Investigated solution pH, adsorbent dosage, initial antibiotic concentration, contact time, temperature and salt effects.
- Conditions recorded in the existing extraction handoff:
  - pH investigated: 3–11.
  - Adsorbent dosage: 0.25–5 g L⁻¹.
  - Contact time: 0–120 min.
  - Initial concentration: 10–150 mg L⁻¹.
  - Salt tests included NaCl up to 0.5 M and KCl or CaCl₂ up to 0.1 M.
- The study analyses adsorption isotherms, kinetics and thermodynamics.
- The reported interpretation in the handoff is that PSO gave the best kinetic fit; Langmuir was suitable for TC and Dubinin–Radushkevich (D–R) for CIP; thermodynamic analysis indicated spontaneous and endothermic adsorption.
- Use this paper for adsorbent classification (raw biomass/waste), influencing factors, comparative TC/CIP behaviour, quantitative evaluation and thermodynamic discussion (slides 6, 8–12).

**Quantitative interpretation guardrail:** The transferred record above preserves model-fit conclusions and experimental ranges, not an independently revalidated set of equilibrium capacities. Do not infer mechanism from PSO fit alone. Do not confuse fitted model parameters with observed uptake or removal percentage.

**Access qualification:** The article refers to Supporting Information available separately; it was not included in the uploaded PDF set. No SI-only details should be asserted.

**Locator status:** Exact page/table/figure locators for the above conditions and model outcomes were not carried over; attach them from the PDF before slide-level quantitative citation.

## REF-10 — Egbedina et al. (2021): ZnO-coated kaolinite/coconut-husk hybrid biochar for simultaneous CIP/TET removal

**Source role:** Direct experimental study of simultaneous ciprofloxacin (CIP) and tetracycline (TET) adsorption.

**Existing extraction/findings preserved**
- Adsorbent platform: kaolinite/coconut-husk hybrid biochar; the reported preparation includes HCl or KOH activation and ZnO coating.
- Evaluates simultaneous CIP and TET removal and includes material characterisation, batch adsorption, isotherm, kinetic and thermodynamic analyses.
- Batch conditions recorded in the existing extraction handoff: 50 mg adsorbent in 10 mL solution; concentration range 10–150 mg L⁻¹; 2 h batch contact.
- Kinetic experiment scale recorded: 500 mg adsorbent per 100 mL solution.
- Abstract-level adsorption-capacity values preserved in the handoff:
  - CIP: 71, 140 and 229 mg g⁻¹ for KCB, KCB-A and KCB-B, respectively.
  - TET: 118, 117 and 232 mg g⁻¹ for KCB, KCB-A and KCB-B, respectively.
  These are reported abstract values and must be checked against the relevant results table and the authors' precise definition (experimental/derived versus fitted capacity) before ranking or plotting.
- Relevant to engineered/composite adsorbents, simultaneous antibiotic adsorption, and comparison of adsorbent preparation/activation effects (slides 6–8, 10–11).

**Source anomaly preserved:** The abstract's kinetic-model sentence repeats the material label “KCB-B” in a sequence where a different label may have been intended. The handoff explicitly required checking the full results/tables rather than silently correcting that apparent discrepancy. This transfer does not resolve it.

**Access qualification:** The article refers to Electronic Supplementary Information (ESI), which was not included in the uploaded PDF. Do not infer ESI contents.

**Locator status:** The exact table/figure/page locator and capacity-type classification for the six abstract values were not carried over; verify from the article before quantitative slide use.
