## Scope and scientific-use notes

- REF-11 is particularly useful for explaining that adsorption/retention and pollutant release or mobility must both be considered. The authors report batch and stirred-flow-chamber experiments; the abstract reports the relative retention/release outcomes for hemp waste, oak ash and mussel shell.
- REF-12 provides a strong teaching example linking adsorbent modification, magnetic recovery, multiple adsorption interactions and regeneration. Its graphical abstract on page 2 depicts cotton husk, γ-Fe2O3, reductive calcination/ball milling, magnetic separation and the two antibiotics (OTC and CIP).
- REF-13 provides a clear schematic teaching example. Its graphical abstract on page 2 depicts hydrogen bonding, electrostatic force, π–π interaction and pore filling around the NiFe2O4/biochar composite. These should be explained as proposed/contributing interactions, not assumed to be exclusive mechanisms.
- REF-14 is useful because the reported kinetic fits are Elovich and Bangham, while the best equilibrium fits are Freundlich, Redlich–Peterson and Sips; this provides a useful contrast with the common Langmuir/PSO examples in other papers.
- REF-15 is a waste-to-adsorbent case study. Its abstract gives a Langmuir-fitted maximum capacity of 1096.871 mg/g and describes pseudo-second-order and Langmuir fits. These are model-fit interpretations, not direct proof of chemisorption or a homogeneous monolayer mechanism; present the authors' interpretations with appropriate qualification.
